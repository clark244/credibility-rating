K-12 edtech company evidence of effectiveness and credibility of marketing claims

A proof of concept from Cobalt Collective. Each row rates one vendor on four separate measures: the strength of the evidence base, how closely its marketing claims match the research (one tile per claim), and its evidence commitment in two parts, outcome studies and ongoing monitoring. The four are not combined into a score; they often point in different directions. Ratings describe claims, not vendors' intent, and come from public materials reviewed in November 2026. Vendor names are withheld; the point is the method, not the verdicts.

How to read the tiles. Each tile is one distinct claim found in the vendor's marketing, colored by how well it matches the evidence; outlined tiles are headline claims. By default, rows are ordered by the share of claims rated Accurate or Mostly accurate; select Vendor, Strength of evidence, Accurate or mostly accurate, or Outcome studies to re-sort (select again to reverse). Strength sorts by each vendor's highest rating. Vendors that make many specific, checkable claims (Vendor I: 50) have more claims that can be found overstated; vague claims are rated Not evaluable instead. Small claim counts (Vendor J: 11 evaluable) make shares fragile. Claim text is abbreviated, with product and place names removed.

Outcome studies, low to high: None (no outcome studies); In-house (at least one pre-post study or stronger, run and reported by vendor staff, limitations stated); Single (one rigorous outcome study with a comparison group, not necessarily independent); Repeated (at least two comparison-group studies, credibly reported, not necessarily independent); Sustained (multiple studies over time, at least two independent, more underway or announced, results published regardless of outcome). A commissioned study counts as independent when an outside organization ran it and the full report is public, including null results. Ongoing monitoring lists what the vendor's public materials show, using these labels. Implementation: usage tracking (logins, minutes, lessons completed), fidelity tracking (delivery against a defined standard), practice tracking (measures of teaching quality), data used to adjust (documented changes made because of the data). Outcomes: vendor outcome measure (a student outcome measure with stated validity evidence), benchmarked growth (reported against norms or expected growth), outcomes linked to implementation (dose related to outcomes in a district's own data, or fees tied to outcomes). "Not described" means public materials don't show it; vendors may do more than they describe.

Badges. "Impact ratings" are ESSA tiers and other outcome-based ratings; "(commissioned)" marks certificates the vendor paid for. "Other reviews" certify program design, content quality or state approval, not impact. Checked against Evidence for ESSA, the Education Endowment Foundation, the CASEL Program Guide, EdReports and vendor sites.
