Vendor E: buyer evidence reportSample

PreK–12 literacy software and teacher PD. Names, places, and sources are withheld in this sample; study-level findings and technical notes are omitted. Rated November 12, 2026 from the vendor’s website, the three studies it cites as ESSA evidence, and outside sources. Not yet re-rated by a second reviewer.

Conflicts of interest. None known to the analyst; to be confirmed before external use.

Buyer summary

ESSA tier. Evidence for ESSA rates the elementary reading program Moderate (four studies) and, for struggling readers, Promising; the secondary reading program Promising; and the English learner program Moderate. No third-party rating was found for the two teacher PD courses. A UK evidence clearinghouse designates the elementary program a Promising Programme.

Marketing claims. About half of claims (16 of 32) are in line with the evidence or modestly stretch it. The other 16, including 10 of 15 headlines, go further than the evidence supports. Marketing notes some limitations.

Verdict. Independent randomized trials abroad support modest gains (about two months’ extra progress) from the elementary reading program for struggling early readers, and a commissioned US study found a small advantage in grades 3–5. Evidence for the secondary reading program rests on small developer-led trials, and results for the PD course on student reading are mixed.

Supported

  • Elementary program: about two months’ extra reading progress for struggling early readers, in two independent randomized trials.
  • English learner program: higher English proficiency and literacy scores in one quasi-experiment.
  • PD course: gains in teacher knowledge of reading instruction.

Weigh cautiously

  • “ESSA Strong” labels: the third-party ratings are Moderate and Promising.
  • Secondary program “up to 5x”: one outcome in a six-class study.
  • PD course effects on student reading: positive in one state, mostly null in another and in an earlier federal trial.
  • Second PD course: no outcome study yet.

Evaluation commitment

Sustained. Two independent, pre-registered randomized trials abroad, a commissioned US evaluation published in full, and state-funded studies of the PD course, including one with mostly null results that the vendor links to.

Ask

  1. Would you update pages that still cite “Strong” Evidence for ESSA ratings?
  2. Which studies support the “2.5x” and “35%” figures?
  3. Are independent US trials of the elementary and secondary programs planned, and will results be published whatever they show?

If you adopt: Compare reading growth for students who meet usage targets with similar non-users on your interim assessment, and track usage by school.

Vendor and reference-check questions

  3. Why do your evidence pages not cite the independent randomized trials abroad of the elementary program?
  4. How was “up to 5x” calculated, and does it hold across all three outcomes in the cited study?

  6. Are independent US trials planned, and do you commit to publishing results whatever they show?
